### Answers to the three questions in plan §22 (both arms)

**Q1 — can the model predict where annotators disagree? Yes.** The head beats the
image-only trivial control in both arms: 0.6172 vs 0.5453 (affine, +0.07) and
0.6087 vs 0.4308 (fixed, +0.18). This is the project's clearest positive result.

> Do not compare against 0.654. That number is the boundary band of the *ground
> truth* majority mask, which is an oracle and not available at inference. An
> earlier version of this analysis used it and wrongly concluded Q1 was negative.

**Q2 — does alignment improve where uncertainty lands? Weakly yes, once the fcomb
is fixed.** In the affine arm `full` gained +0.024 `unc_corr` but paid 12% dice
and worse GED — a wash. In the fixed arm the same +0.026 gain comes with the
*best* GED of the three variants and only a 4% dice cost. The effect is small
either way, and saturates in λ_A, but it stops being self-defeating once the
latent can vary spatially.

**Q3 — is segmentation quality preserved? Yes.** In the fixed arm `baseline`
leads on dice (0.3552) and `full` on GED (0.3884), spanning ~4%. In the affine
arm `head` matched baseline while `full` lost 12% dice.

## 3. Why the Q2 null was hard to interpret, and what we did about it

Three separate defects, all confirmed empirically:

1. **The fcomb was affine.** `InjectionUNet.forward` registered
   `reduce-{i}-nonlin` activations but never called them, so the 1×1 stack was one
   linear map. With a spatially-constant latent that makes
   `out1 - out0 = S(x,y) + c(z)`, i.e. every sample a level set of one function.
   Measured: **100% of test images had strictly nested samples in all three
   variants** — the latent could only dilate/erode the mask globally, so the
   alignment loss had a single degree of freedom to work with.
   Fixed (`probunet/model.py`, the `reduce-{i}-nonlin` call in `forward`).
2. **The uncertainty metric cannot see diversity.** Plan §9's `U` = entropy of the
   mean of K samples scores 16 identical blurry maps and 16 maximally split crisp
   maps both at 1.0, and it is used as *both* the training signal and the gauge.
   `probunet.disagreement.sample_diversity_from_samples` (mutual information) was
   added as a metric that can. **In practice this mattered less than expected** —
   it tracks entropy-of-mean within 0.01 on these runs, because nested binary
   samples make the two nearly coincide.
3. **The alignment target is unachievable on LIDC.** `D_GT` averages 0.722 over
   the lesion union and 0.359 deep inside the eroded majority mask, because 65% of
   crops have a grader who saw no lesion. So `|U - D_GT|` asks for p ≈ 0.5 across
   whole lesions, fighting the segmentation loss. This is why dice collapses
   0.30 → 0.12 at λ_A = 1e-3. **Not fixed** — it is a property of the objective on
   this dataset, and is the most promising thing to change next (see §6).

## 4. The fcomb-fixed arm (100k steps) — complete

`outputs_fcombfix/`, λ_D = 0.01, λ_A = 1e-5, all three variants finished.

| head | 0.3079 | 0.2407 | 0.4025 | 0.4923 | 0.4889 | 0.6087 | 0.6872 | 3% |

Trivial control for Q1 in this arm: **0.4308**.

**Use this table, not `outputs_fcombfix/final_eval/`.** That one selects
`best_checkpoint.pt`, and best-GED selection lands at step 1000 (baseline) and
3000 (head) here — barely-trained models, head at dice 0.18 — against step 71000
for `full`. It compares different amounts of training, not different methods.

### What changed once the fcomb was fixed

**Nestedness collapsed from 100% to 1–3%**, with higher diversity (E[d(S,S')]
0.69 vs 0.57). The architectural constraint is gone.

**Q2 flips from null to weakly positive.** `unc_corr` now orders consistently —
baseline 0.4828 < head 0.4923 < full 0.5086 — and, unlike the affine arm, `full`
no longer pays for it: it has the **best GED of the three** (0.3884 vs 0.3939)
and costs only 4% dice against baseline, versus 12% in the affine arm. The +0.026
margin is similar in size to before, but it is now free rather than bought.

**Q1 strengthens.** `head` 0.6087 and `full` 0.6270 against a 0.4308 control —
a margin of +0.18 to +0.20, much wider than the +0.07 in the affine arm.

### The one comparison you cannot make

Absolute GED is worse than the affine arm (0.388 vs 0.316) — but the two arms
**do not share a learning-rate schedule**. `set_lr` derives its decay interval
from `--steps`, so the 100k runs decay every 20k while the 240k runs decayed every
48k; by step 40k the 100k arm is at lr 1.6e-5 while the 240k arm was still at
1e-4. The 100k arm plateaus early because its learning rate collapsed, which is
not evidence about the fcomb.

**To settle it, run the affine arm at 100k** (3 × ~3.7h) so both arms share a
schedule — cheaper than rerunning the fixed arm at 240k, and still the single most
valuable next experiment.

## 4b. The distribution-matching arm (100k steps) — complete, negative

`outputs_distribution/`, branch `multi-rater-distribution-matching`.
`--variant distribution` drops the ELBO entirely (no random-grader CE, no
posterior, no KL) and trains K=4 gradient-carrying prior samples against the four
masks with a two-way soft-Dice loss, plus a consensus term and the same L_D.
Two aggregations, both run: `--distribution-mode coverage` (plan §8.2) and
`kernel` (§8.3), λ_dist 1.0 / λ_con 0.2 / λ_D 0.01 / τ 0.1.

Test set, matched 100k `latest_checkpoint.pt`, in
`outputs_distribution/final_eval_100k/`:

| variant | dice | IoU | GED | U–D corr | pred D corr | control | E[d(S,S')] | nested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| coverage | **0.4383** | **0.3688** | 0.7135 | 0.1201 | 0.5645 | 0.5065 | 0.0239 | 99.5% |
| kernel | 0.4051 | 0.3367 | 0.9329 | 0.1033 | 0.5689 | 0.4260 | 0.0004 | 100% |

**Best dice of any arm, worst GED of any arm, and the reason is one column.**
`E[d(S,S')]` is 0.024 / 0.0004 against 0.69 for the fcomb-fixed arm, with
99–100% of images nested: all 16 samples are the same mask. The model is
deterministic.

Why: the two-way soft-Dice objective plus the consensus term is minimised by
putting *every* sample on the single mask closest to all four annotations —
nothing in it rewards spread. In the ELBO the KL term is what keeps the prior
wide; deleting the CVAE deleted the only pressure holding samples apart. The
dice win is circular (trained on soft Dice, evaluated by Dice, on what is now a
point estimate).

Two things worth carrying forward:

- **Do not use disagreement MAE as a primary metric.** This arm has the *lowest*
  `unc_mae` (0.0125 vs 0.0176 for `full`) with essentially no correlation, because
  D_GT is ~0 almost everywhere and "no uncertainty anywhere" scores well on MAE.
- The idea itself is close to **MoSE (Gao et al., ICLR 2023)**, which matches a
  segmentation distribution to the annotation set with a Wasserstein-like loss on
  LIDC — but MoSE keeps an explicit mixture with a gating network, i.e. structural
  diversity the loss cannot collapse. Reviving this arm needs an explicit
  diversity/repulsion term or the KL retained, not a different τ.

`scripts/eval_lidc_final.py` needed two changes to evaluate it: the head gate now
includes `"distribution"`, and rows are labelled by run directory so `coverage`
and `kernel` do not both print as `distribution`.

## 5. Numerical stability — three things that will bite you

All three only appeared once the fcomb nonlinearity was enabled. All are fixed in
the code, but you need to know they exist before changing hyperparameters.

1. **Loss reduction.** `criterion = nn.NLLLoss(reduction="sum")` sums over batch
   *and* pixels, so gradients are ~32× the paper's per-image convention. Without
   `--grad-clip` (default 100) **all three variants NaN in the prior encoder
   within ~2k steps.**
2. **λ_A = 1e-4 diverges.** The alignment loss backprops into the prior through
   `sample_prior_train`, and inflating the prior variance is the cheapest way to
   raise sample diversity. KL doubles every step until overflow. 1e-5 is stable
   (KL settles ~43). **The fixed arm therefore uses 1e-5 while the affine arm used
   1e-4 — a second variable between the arms.**
3. **The prior must be detached in `sample_prior_train`.** Even at λ_A = 1e-5,
   `full` NaN'd again at ~4–5k steps: logvar drifts up until `exp(0.5·logvar)`
   overflows, which gradient clipping cannot prevent because the forward pass
   produces the inf.

   A `logvar.clamp(±10)` was tried first and **made things much worse** — dice
   0.020 and GED 1.44 at step 1000, versus 0.30 / 0.35 unclamped. `torch.clamp`
   has zero gradient outside its range, so once logvar saturates the KL term can
   no longer pull the variance back down; the clamp converts a divergence into an
   inescapable dead zone. The clamp is still in `model.py` as a cheap safety net
   (`LOGVAR_MIN/MAX`), but it must never be the thing doing the work.

   The actual fix is in `sample_prior_train`, which now detaches the prior's
   parameters before sampling. The alignment loss should shape how the *decoder*
   turns z into a segmentation; letting it reach the prior means the cheapest way
   to raise sample diversity is to inflate the prior variance without bound. With
   the prior detached, KL alone governs it — as in Kohl et al. Recovered to dice
   0.299 / GED 0.571 at step 1000.

   `full` then trained cleanly for the full 100k (6.6h), with its best GED at
   step 71000 — a genuinely late optimum, unlike baseline (step 1000) and head
   (step 3000) in the same arm.

Also note the λ values are not the plan's. Because `L_D` and `L_align` are summed
over pixels to match the reconstruction, plan §7/§13's λ ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0} would
put the auxiliary terms at 0.6×–6× the reconstruction. The working range is
λ_D ≈ 0.01, λ_A ≈ 1e-5.

## 6. What we would do next, in priority order

0. **Report the fcomb-fixed arm (§4) as the headline result** — that decision
   is made; see `overleaf/main.tex`. §4b is reported alongside it as a negative
   result, not buried.
1. **Rerun the affine arm at 100k** to de-confound §4. Three runs, ~3.7h each.
   This is the one experiment that would let you say whether the fcomb fix helps
   or hurts absolute GED.
2. **Boundary-restricted alignment.** Defect (3) in §3 is the real conceptual
   problem: on LIDC, disagreement is largely about lesion *existence*, so matching
   `U` to `D_GT` demands maximal uncertainty over whole lesions. Restricting
   `L_align` to a boundary band would let it shape edge placement without fighting
   the segmentation loss. This is the most likely route to a positive Q2.
3. **Mutual-info alignment** (`--uncertainty-measure` exists on branch
   `disagreement_probunet_v2`) — lower priority now that we know the metric tracks
   entropy-of-mean closely here.
4. The λ sweep in plan §7/§13 is **not** worth running: the effect saturates at
   ~+0.02 `unc_corr` regardless of λ_A while the dice cost grows.

## 7. Known deviations from the paper (Appendix H.1)

- Orthogonal init (gain 1) is never applied — `ConvModule.init_weights` exists but
  nothing calls it.
- LR ends at 2.51e-6 rather than 1e-6: `decay_index` is capped one step short.
- Augmentation is crop-only; no elastic / rotation / shear / scale.
- β is effectively 1/32 relative to the paper, because the reconstruction is
  summed over the batch rather than averaged.

## 8. Getting the checkpoints

`*.pt` files are gitignored — 29 files, ~5.9 GB total, 217 MB each. Everything in
this document except the weights themselves is reproducible from git.

If you need the trained weights rather than retraining:

- **Preferred — retrain.** `bash scripts/run_ablation.sh <gpu> <variant>`.
  ~3.7h for baseline/head, ~6h for full, on one GPU each. Seeds are fixed
  (`--seed 1`) and all hyperparameters are in each run's `args.json`, which *is*
  in git, so runs are reproducible.
- **Direct transfer.** `rsync -avP <host>:<repo>/outputs_*/lidc_ablation/*/best_checkpoint.pt .`
  if you have SSH access to the machine that trained them.
- **Cloud storage.** Upload to Google Drive / S3 and share a link; do not try to
  commit them.
- **Git LFS** would work but is overkill for a course project and needs LFS quota
  on the remote.

The three checkpoints actually worth transferring are the affine-arm
`best_checkpoint.pt` files under `outputs_12_08_26_240000/lidc_ablation/`, since
those produced the headline table in §2. Everything else can be retrained.

Each checkpoint contains `model_state_dict`, `optimizer_state_dict`, `step`,
`args`, `metrics`, and full RNG state, so `--resume` restarts exactly.
