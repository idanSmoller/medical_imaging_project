\section*{Title page details}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Selected paper:} S.~Kohl et al., \emph{A Probabilistic U-Net for
        Segmentation of Ambiguous Images}, NeurIPS 2018.
  \item \textbf{Proposed extension:} supervise \emph{where} human annotators
        disagree, with (i) a head that predicts the inter-rater disagreement map
        from the image alone and (ii) a loss that pushes the model's own sample
        diversity into those same pixels.
  \item \textbf{Headline outcome:} the head clearly works ($+0.20$ correlation
        over a trivial control); the alignment loss gives a small but consistent
        gain ($+0.026$) at the best generalized energy distance of our arms. A
        fourth arm that replaces the paper's objective with direct
        distribution matching wins on Dice but collapses to zero sample
        diversity --- a negative result we report and explain.
\end{itemize}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{The problem, for a reader new to the task.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Segmentation labels every pixel (here: is this pixel part of a lung
        nodule?), and training normally assumes one correct mask per image. In
        medical imaging that assumption is false: experts outline the same lesion
        differently, and on LIDC-IDRI they sometimes disagree about whether a
        lesion is there at all.
  \item A standard U-Net trained on one expert's mask, or on their average, emits
        a single confident mask and destroys that information.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the paper does.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The Probabilistic U-Net combines a U-Net with a conditional VAE. A prior
        network maps the image $x$ to a distribution $p(z\mid x)$ over a
        low-dimensional latent (6-D here); every draw of $z$ decodes to a
        different plausible segmentation --- a \emph{coherent whole mask}. That is
        the paper's central design choice: it argues explicitly against pixel-wise
        probability maps, because they cannot express which pixels vary
        \emph{together}.
  \item Training minimises the negative ELBO. A posterior $q(z\mid x,Y_g)$
        additionally sees \emph{one randomly chosen} expert mask $Y_g$:
        \[
          L_{\text{PU-Net}}
          = \underbrace{\mathrm{E}_{z\sim q(z\mid x,Y_g)}\!\left[-\log p(Y_g\mid x,z)\right]}
              _{\text{reconstruction (cross-entropy, summed over pixels)}}
            \;+\; \beta\,\underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q(z\mid x,Y_g)\,\|\,p(z\mid x)\right)}
              _{\text{pulls the prior towards the posterior}} .
        \]
        The balance between the two terms is not cosmetic: Section~5 describes how
        getting the reconstruction's reduction wrong collapses the posterior onto
        the prior and destroys sampling entirely.
  \item Sampling $z$ repeatedly therefore yields a \emph{distribution} of
        segmentations, scored against the four expert masks with the
        \textbf{generalized energy distance}. Writing $S,S'$ for independent
        model samples and $Y,Y'$ for independent grader masks,
        \[
          D^2_{\mathrm{GED}}
          = 2\,\mathrm{E}\!\left[d(S,Y)\right]
            - \underbrace{\mathrm{E}\!\left[d(S,S')\right]}_{\text{sample diversity}}
            - \mathrm{E}\!\left[d(Y,Y')\right],
          \qquad d = 1-\mathrm{IoU} .
        \]
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why it matters, and why we chose it.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item In nodule screening the disputed cases are the clinically interesting
        ones: a model that says ``experts would argue \emph{here}'' is more useful
        as a second reader than one that draws a confident wrong boundary. LIDC
        ships \textbf{four} expert masks per image, so disagreement is directly
        measurable --- rare among segmentation datasets.
  \item \textbf{The gap --- read the middle term of $D^2_{\mathrm{GED}}$ twice.}
        Every term of GED is a \emph{scalar}: $\mathrm{E}[d(S,S')]$ says how
        \emph{much} the model's samples vary and $\mathrm{E}[d(Y,Y')]$ how much the
        graders vary, but nothing in it says \emph{where}. A model could put the
        right \emph{amount} of variability in the wrong \emph{places} and score
        just as well. The ``where'' question needs the two \emph{maps} behind those
        scalars --- $U$ for the samples, $\Dgt$ for the graders (Section~2) --- and
        comparing them pixel by pixel is precisely what we add.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed extension}

\paragraph{Three quantities, kept distinct throughout the report.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Dgt$ --- \emph{human} disagreement, computed from the four masks.
        With $P_i=\frac{1}{G}\sum_g Y_i^{(g)}$ the per-pixel mean over $G=4$
        graders, $\Dgt$ is its normalized binary entropy
        \[
          \Dgt_i=-\frac{P_i\log(P_i+\epsilon)+(1-P_i)\log(1-P_i+\epsilon)}{\log 2}\in[0,1],
        \]
        so $\Dgt=0$ where all four agree and $1$ where they split two-two.
  \item $\hat D$ --- the \emph{prediction} of $\Dgt$ made by our new head.
  \item $U$ --- \emph{model} uncertainty. Draw $K$ latents from the prior, decode
        each to a foreground-probability map $Q^{(k)}$, and average them into
        $\bar Q_i=\frac{1}{K}\sum_k Q_i^{(k)}$. Then $U$ is the \emph{same
        functional} as $\Dgt$, with the $K$ samples playing the role of the $G$
        graders:
        \[
          U_i=-\frac{\bar Q_i\log(\bar Q_i+\epsilon)+(1-\bar Q_i)\log(1-\bar Q_i+\epsilon)}{\log 2}.
        \]
        Using one functional on both sides is what makes ``$U\approx\Dgt$'' a
        claim about \emph{where} uncertainty sits rather than a comparison of
        two different units.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What we add (two pieces, both minimal on purpose).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{(B) Disagreement head.} One $1{\times}1$ convolution $+$ sigmoid
        on the last U-Net feature map, tapped \emph{before} $z$ is injected, so
        $\hat D$ is a function of the image alone. Trained with
        $L_D=\mathrm{MSE}(\hat D,\Dgt)$.
  \item \textbf{(C) Alignment loss.} $L_{\text{align}}=\frac{1}{HW}\sum_i|U_i-\Dgt_i|$,
        penalising predictive diversity that lands anywhere other than where the
        graders differ. Full objective
        $L=L_{\text{PU-Net}}+\lambda_D L_D+\lambda_A L_{\text{align}}$; in
        Figure~\ref{fig:model} everything in the dashed box is new.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Where our addition sits: a marginal readout on a joint model.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Dgt$ and $U$ are per-pixel \emph{marginals} --- projections of the joint
        distribution over masks. We do not swap one object for the other: the CVAE
        still owns the joint (the KL keeps the prior wide and the samples
        coherent), and $L_{\text{align}}$ only says \emph{where} the marginal
        variance should sit. $U$ is computed from the $K$ decoded samples, so it
        is a \emph{readout} of that distribution, not a parallel pixel-wise model.
  \item Four masks pin the per-pixel Bernoulli down exactly --- with $G=4$, $\Dgt$
        can only take the values $\{0,\,0.81,\,1\}$ --- but say very little about
        the joint. A marginal constraint is roughly all the labels can support.
  \item \textbf{Caveat we state rather than gloss:} $\hat D$ \emph{is} a pixel-wise
        predictor, a $1{\times}1$ conv on image features with no sample involved.
        It is an auxiliary output \emph{alongside} the sample-based segmentation,
        never a substitute for it.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Hypothesis, as two testable claims.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Q1.} Where annotators will disagree is predictable from the image
        alone: $\hat D\approx\Dgt$, beating a trivial ``just outline the lesion''
        control.
  \item \textbf{Q2.} Adding $L_{\text{align}}$ raises the agreement between $U$
        and $\Dgt$ over the baseline, without damaging segmentation quality or GED.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Alternatives we considered.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Condition the prior on disagreement}, $p(z\mid x,\hat D)$.
        Rejected: $\hat D$ is itself a deterministic function of $x$, so it adds
        no information and confounds the ablation.
  \item \textbf{A dedicated multi-rater architecture.} MRNet~\cite{ji2021}
        models rater (dis)agreement with an expertise-aware module and extra
        branches. Rejected for this project: with a single $1{\times}1$
        convolution, any gain is attributable to the \emph{supervision signal}
        rather than to added capacity.
  \item \textbf{Per-rater heads / confusion matrices}~\cite{zhang2020}. Rejected:
        the LIDC release does not identify which radiologist produced which mask
        and the four raters differ across scans, so a per-rater parameterisation
        has nothing stable to learn.
  \item \textbf{Replace the ELBO with direct distribution matching.} Treat the
        four masks as an empirical distribution and train the $K$ samples to match
        it with a two-way soft-Dice loss --- close in spirit to
        MoSE~\cite{gao2023}, which matches a segmentation distribution to the
        annotation set with a Wasserstein-like loss on LIDC. Rather than only
        discussing it, \textbf{we implemented and ran it as a fourth arm} (D).
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology}

\paragraph{Model.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Faithful PyTorch re-implementation of the Probabilistic U-Net (the
        official repo is old TensorFlow and does not run on current Colab):
        U-Net backbone with base 32 channels and 4 down/up-samplings, prior
        encoder, posterior encoder, 6-D latent, and \texttt{fcomb}-style
        injection of $z$ into the final $1{\times}1$ convolution stack.
  \item Our additions live in a subclass, so the baseline stays runnable for a
        controlled comparison.
  \item \textbf{A bug in our own re-implementation, worth reporting.} The final
        $1{\times}1$ stack initially had no nonlinearity between its convolutions,
        so it collapsed to a single affine map; since the injected $z$ is
        spatially constant, every sample became a level set of one function and
        $z$ could only globally grow or shrink one mask. Measured effect:
        \textbf{100\% of test images had strictly nested samples.} Adding the
        missing activation dropped that to 1--3\%, and is what let any
        diversity-shaping loss work at all.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Data.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item LIDC-IDRI 2D crops, DeepMind's preprocessed release (CC BY 3.0)
        published with the Hierarchical Probabilistic U-Net~\cite{kohl2019};
        preprocessing follows the original paper's Appendix H.1.
  \item Splits: 8843 train / 1993 val / 1980 test images (530 / 111 / 103
        patients), $180\times180$ 8-bit grayscale, four masks each. We take a
        random $128\times128$ crop, scale images to $[0,1]$ and binarise masks.
        These counts are $\sim$0.4\% below the 8882 / 1996 / 1992 quoted in the
        paper, because the authors cleaned the data after publication and the 2018
        snapshot was never released; we therefore cite the HPU-Net release and do
        not claim a byte-identical reproduction.
  \item \textbf{Roughly 65\% of training crops contain at least one empty mask}
        --- graders disagree about lesion \emph{existence}, not only about the
        boundary. This is signal, not corruption, and it is why the
        \emph{empty-mask convention} defined in Section~5 matters.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Loss: summed cross-entropy reconstruction $+\ \beta\,\mathrm{KL}$
        ($\beta=1$, 6-D latent, per Appendix H.1), plus $\lambda_D L_D$ and
        $\lambda_A L_{\text{align}}$ for arms B and C.
  \item Adam, lr $10^{-4}$ decayed to $10^{-6}$ in 5 steps, batch 32, weight
        decay $10^{-5}$, gradient-norm clipping at 100, $K=4$ samples per step,
        16 samples per image at test time; augmentation is the random translated
        crop only.
  \item \textbf{100k steps per arm} (the paper uses 240k), one GPU,
        $\approx$3.7\,h for A/B, $\approx$6.6\,h for C, $\approx$6.0\,h for D.
  \item $\lambda_D=0.01$ and $\lambda_A=10^{-5}$; both are load-bearing.
        $L_D$ is summed over pixels, so $\lambda_D=0.5$ makes it $\sim$3$\times$
        the reconstruction term; and $\lambda_A=10^{-4}$ diverges within 2k steps
        because inflating the prior variance is the cheapest way to raise
        diversity (Section~5).
  \item Tools: Python, PyTorch, NumPy, Matplotlib. The submitted code runs in
        Google Colab: a self-contained notebook executes the whole pipeline
        end-to-end in a reduced ``smoke'' mode, and the same code paths, run at
        full length on one GPU, produced every number below.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Ablation design (one variable at a time).}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Arm & Disagreement head & Alignment loss & Objective \\
\midrule
A -- baseline PU-Net & no  & no  & $L_{\text{PU-Net}}$ \\
B -- $+$ head        & yes & no  & $+\lambda_D L_D$ \\
C -- full model      & yes & yes & $+\lambda_D L_D+\lambda_A L_{\text{align}}$ \\[2pt]
D -- distribution matching & yes & no &
  $\lambda_{\text{dist}}L_{\text{dist}}+\lambda_{\text{con}}L_{\text{con}}+\lambda_D L_D$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Arm D in detail.} The ELBO is dropped entirely (no random-grader
        cross-entropy, no posterior, no KL). Instead $K$ samples are drawn from
        the prior \emph{with gradients}, and a $K\times G$ matrix of soft-Dice
        distances to the four masks is reduced two ways:
        \emph{coverage} (every human mask must be matched by some sample and
        vice versa, via a soft minimum) or \emph{kernel} (a two-way soft kernel
        likelihood). $L_{\text{con}}$ is a soft Dice between the mean sample and
        the mean human mask. Both modes are reported.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results and analysis}

\paragraph{Metrics, and what each is for.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dice / IoU} --- sanity check that the extension does not damage
        segmentation quality, computed on the deterministic prediction against
        each of the four masks. \emph{Not} the headline claim.
  \item \textbf{Generalized energy distance (GED)}, the paper's own metric,
        defined in Section~1: our 16 samples against the four human masks, with
        $d=1-\mathrm{IoU}$. We evaluate it under the \textbf{empty-mask
        convention} of the paper's Appendix~B --- $d\equiv0$ when both masks are
        empty, so agreeing that there is no lesion is rewarded rather than
        punished. We apply the same convention to Dice/IoU by excluding
        empty-vs-empty pairs instead of scoring them 0; without it the 65\% of
        crops with an empty mask deflate every model and hide real differences.
  \item \textbf{$\mathrm{corr}(U,\Dgt)$} --- does the model become uncertain in
        the same places humans disagree? \textbf{This is the primary result.}
  \item \textbf{Trivial control} --- disagreement in LIDC sits largely on lesion
        boundaries, so a map that merely marks the boundary already correlates with
        $\Dgt$. Ours is the 5\,px \emph{boundary band} (dilation $-$ erosion) of
        \textbf{the model's own predicted mask} --- roughly 2\,px inside and 2\,px
        outside its edge. It uses no grader information whatever, so unlike a band
        drawn around the ground truth it is available at inference. $\hat D$ must
        beat it to have learned anything about \emph{raters} rather than about
        outlines.
  \item \textbf{Diversity diagnostics} --- the subtracted $\mathrm{E}[d(S,S')]$
        term of GED, reported on its own, and the fraction of images whose samples
        are strictly nested. Together they separate ``diverse in the wrong place''
        from ``not diverse at all''.
\end{itemize}

all arms read from the matched 100k-step checkpoint.

\paragraph{Q1 --- can the model predict where annotators will disagree? Yes.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The head reaches $\mathrm{corr}(\hat D,\Dgt)=0.627$ against its own
        boundary-band control of $0.425$: \textbf{$+0.20$}. It has learned
        something about rater behaviour, not merely where lesion edges are. This is the
        project's clearest positive result and it holds in every arm with a head.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Q2 --- does the alignment loss move uncertainty to the right place? Weakly yes.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathrm{corr}(U,\Dgt)$ rises $0.483\rightarrow0.509$ from A to C, and C
        simultaneously attains the \emph{best} GED of the three ELBO arms
        (0.388), for a 4\% relative Dice cost.
  \item The gain is modest, and we read that as \emph{structural} rather than a
        tuning failure: $L_{\text{align}}$ constrains a marginal of a joint object,
        so two sample distributions with identical marginals are invisible to it.
        It can relocate variance; it cannot reshape modes.
  \item It only became visible at all after the \texttt{fcomb} fix of Section~3:
        with 100\% nested samples $U$ is a single ring around one mask, and no loss
        can reshape it.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Q3 --- and arm D: direct distribution matching wins Dice and loses everything else.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Arm D posts the best Dice by a wide margin (0.438 vs.\ 0.355) and the
        worst GED by a wide margin (0.714 / 0.933 vs.\ 0.388).
  \item \textbf{The mechanism is in one column:} $\mathrm{E}[d(S,S')]$ is
        \textbf{0.024} (coverage) and \textbf{0.0004} (kernel), against 0.69 for
        arms A--C, with \textbf{99--100\% of test images nested}. All 16 samples
        are effectively the same mask --- the model has become deterministic, and
        the subtracted diversity term of $D^2_{\mathrm{GED}}$ vanishes, which is
        exactly why GED nearly doubles while Dice improves.
  \item \textbf{Why.} The two-way soft-Dice objective plus the consensus term is
        minimised by placing \emph{every} sample on the single mask closest to all
        four annotations. Nothing in it rewards spread. In the original ELBO the
        KL term keeps the prior wide; deleting the CVAE machinery deleted the only
        pressure holding the samples apart.
  \item The Dice win is therefore circular: arm D is trained on a soft-Dice
        criterion and then evaluated by Dice on what is now a single consensus
        mask. It is a better \emph{point} estimate and a far worse model of
        ambiguity, which is the thing the paper set out to capture.
  \item \textbf{Arm D is the mirror image of our extension.} It tries to obtain the
        joint \emph{from the loss} --- matching whole masks set-to-set --- after
        deleting the machinery that maintained it, and ends up with no joint at
        all. We keep the CVAE's joint and constrain only its marginal.
  \item \textbf{A metric lesson worth stating.} Arm D also achieves the
        \emph{lowest} disagreement MAE (0.0125 vs.\ 0.0176 for C), because $\Dgt$
        is near zero almost everywhere and predicting ``no uncertainty anywhere''
        scores well on a mean absolute error. Correlation exposes this
        immediately; MAE does not. We report both and rely on correlation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Failure cases and challenges encountered.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Posterior collapse (first full run, discarded).} Our
        reconstruction term averaged cross-entropy over pixels while Kohl et al.
        sum it; at $128\times128$ that under-weights reconstruction $\sim$16000$\times$
        against $\beta\,\mathrm{KL}$, so $\beta=1$ behaved like $\beta\approx16000$.
        The KL hit exactly 0 by 30k steps, sample diversity fell from 0.45 to
        0.006, and val GED \emph{rose} 0.34$\rightarrow$0.61 while Dice improved.
        Fixed by summing the cross-entropy.
  \item \textbf{Divergence and a runaway prior.} With the corrected (summed) loss
        every arm produced NaNs in the prior encoder within 2k steps, fixed with
        gradient clipping and a $\pm10$ clamp on the latent log-variance. And at
        $\lambda_A=10^{-4}$ the KL doubled every step until overflow, because
        $L_{\text{align}}$ back-propagates into the prior and inflating the prior
        variance is the cheapest way to raise diversity; fixed by detaching the
        prior in the sampling path and using $\lambda_A=10^{-5}$.
  \item \textbf{Model selection.} Selecting the checkpoint on best validation GED
        picks step 1000--3000 for arms A and B --- a barely-trained model with a
        wide, uninformative prior scores well on GED. We therefore report the
        matched 100k-step checkpoint for every arm, which compares methods rather
        than training length.
  \item \textbf{The conceptual limit (Figure~\ref{fig:qual}).} On LIDC much of
        the disagreement is about lesion existence, so matching $U$ to $\Dgt$
        demands maximal uncertainty over \emph{whole} lesions, which directly
        opposes the reconstruction term. This bounds how far $L_{\text{align}}$
        can go, and points at the fix in Section~6.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Q1 is confirmed:} where radiologists will disagree is predictable
        from the image alone --- $0.627$ correlation against a $0.425$ trivial
        control, from a single $1{\times}1$ convolution. \textbf{Q2 is supported
        but modest:} the alignment loss raises $\mathrm{corr}(U,\Dgt)$ by $0.026$
        and gives the best GED of our ELBO arms, at a 4\% Dice cost.
  \item \textbf{The claim we make} is not ``our Dice is higher''. The baseline
        already generates diverse plausible segmentations; our extension makes
        that diversity correspond to where experts actually disagree, and makes
        the disagreement itself an explicit, inspectable output.
  \item \textbf{One sentence for the whole design:} joint structure from the CVAE,
        marginal localisation from our loss. Arm D shows what happens when you try
        to get the joint from the loss instead --- the best point estimate and no
        distribution at all ($\mathrm{E}[d(S,S')]\approx0$, 99--100\% nested).
  \item \textbf{The sharpest limitation is the marginal itself.} $\Dgt$ is a
        per-pixel quantity: two graders drawing mask A and two drawing mask B
        produce exactly the same $\Dgt$ as four graders disagreeing incoherently.
        Neither $\Dgt$ nor $U$ can tell coherent modes from pixel noise, which
        bounds both the supervision and the evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Other limitations:} 100k of the paper's 240k iterations and
        crop-only augmentation, so our absolute GED sits above published values;
        one dataset and anatomy; $\Dgt$ estimated from only four graders; one seed
        per arm, so arm B's Dice gap is not separable from run-to-run noise.
  \item \textbf{Future work:} restrict $L_{\text{align}}$ to a boundary band so it
        shapes edge placement without demanding uncertainty over whole lesions
        --- the most likely route to a strong Q2; rescue arm D by adding an
        explicit diversity/repulsion term or retaining the KL; and compare against
        the Hierarchical Probabilistic U-Net and MoSE on the same split.
\end{itemize}
